# Logical Operators in MySQL

Logical operators are used to combine multiple conditions or relational statements.

The main logical operators in MySQL are:

- `AND`
- `OR`
- `NOT`

---

## AND Operator

The `AND` operator is used when **all conditions must be true**.

If both conditions are true, the result is `TRUE`.

Example:

    SAL > 25000 AND GENDER = 'Female'

Both conditions must be satisfied.

---

## OR Operator

The `OR` operator is used when **at least one condition must be true**.

If any one condition is true, the result is `TRUE`.

Example:

    SAL > 30000 OR GENDER = 'Female'

If either condition is true, the record is selected.

---

## NOT Operator

The `NOT` operator reverses the result of a condition.

- `TRUE` becomes `FALSE`
- `FALSE` becomes `TRUE`

Example:

    NOT SAL = 30000

This means the salary should **not be 30000**.

# Logical Operator Queries

## Q1. Employee Details with Salary Greater Than 25,000 and Gender Female

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE SAL > 25000 AND GENDER = 'Female';

### Explanation

Here, two conditions are used:

    SAL > 25000
    GENDER = 'Female'

Because `AND` is used, **both conditions must be true**.

---

## Q2. Employee Details with Salary Between 30,000 and 40,000

### Using AND

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE SAL > 30000 AND SAL < 40000;

### Using BETWEEN

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE SAL BETWEEN 30000 AND 40000;

### Explanation

`BETWEEN` checks whether a value falls within a specified range.

Important:

    BETWEEN 30000 AND 40000

includes both `30000` and `40000`.

So the following two conditions are different:

    SAL > 30000 AND SAL < 40000

and

    SAL BETWEEN 30000 AND 40000

The first excludes `30000` and `40000`.

The second includes `30000` and `40000`.

---

## Q3. Employees Who Joined After December 12, 2019 OR Have Salary Greater Than 25,000

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE JDATE > '2019-12-12' OR SAL > 25000;

### Explanation

The `OR` operator is used.

An employee is selected if:

- The joining date is after `2019-12-12`, **OR**
- The salary is greater than `25000`

Only one condition needs to be true.

---

## Q4. Employees with Salary Greater Than 25,000 and Gender Male OR Salary Greater Than 30,000

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE (SAL > 25000 AND GENDER = 'Male')
       OR SAL > 30000;

### Explanation

Parentheses are used to clearly group the `AND` condition.

The query checks:

    (SAL > 25000 AND GENDER = 'Male')
    OR
    (SAL > 30000)

Therefore, an employee is selected when either:

1. Salary is greater than `25000` **and** gender is `Male`

**OR**

2. Salary is greater than `30000`

### Why Use Parentheses?

Parentheses make the intended condition clear and help avoid confusion when using multiple `AND` and `OR` operators.

---

## Q5. Employees Who Have NOT Joined on October 22, 2022

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE NOT JDATE = '2022-10-22';

### Alternative

    SELECT *
    FROM EMP_INFO
    WHERE JDATE <> '2022-10-22';

### Explanation

`NOT` reverses the condition.

The condition:

    JDATE = '2022-10-22'

means the employee joined on October 22, 2022.

Using `NOT`:

    NOT JDATE = '2022-10-22'

means the employee did **not** join on that date.

The `<>` operator also means **not equal to**.

Therefore:

    NOT JDATE = '2022-10-22'

and

    JDATE <> '2022-10-22'

produce the same result in this case.
